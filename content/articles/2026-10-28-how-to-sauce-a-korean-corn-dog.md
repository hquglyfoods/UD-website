Sugar on a corn dog sounds wrong right up until the first bite. Then it makes perfect sense.

A Korean corn dog leaves the fryer as a blank canvas: crisp shell, soft batter, savory or cheesy center. What you put on it next changes the whole snack. Here is how the classic finishes work, and how to build your own.

## Why do people put sugar on Korean corn dogs?

Sugar is the original Korean street finish. A light roll in granulated sugar while the shell is still hot gives you a sparkly crunch and a sweet edge that plays against the salty sausage or mild mozzarella inside. It is the same reason salted caramel works. Sweet and salty make each other taste bigger.

If you have never tried it, start with a light dusting instead of a full coat. You can always add more.

## Ketchup and mustard: the zigzag classic

The zigzag of ketchup and yellow mustard is the look most people picture. Ketchup brings sweetness and tang, mustard brings sharp heat, and together they cut through the richness of a fried shell. It is a safe, satisfying pick for a sausage or half and half corn dog.

Tip: drizzle in thin lines, not big puddles. Thin lines keep the shell crisp, while heavy sauce on one spot can soften it.

## The sweet and salty combo

Here is the move regulars love: sugar first, then a zigzag of ketchup on top. You get the sparkle of the sugar, the tang of the ketchup, and the savory center all in one bite. It sounds like a lot, and it is, in the best way.

## Which finish goes with which corn dog?

- **Mozzarella.** Sugar shines here. The mild, milky cheese loves a sweet edge.
- **Sausage.** Ketchup and mustard is the natural pair, and the sugar plus ketchup combo is worth a try.
- **Half and half.** Split the difference: sugar on the cheese end, sauce on the sausage end.
- **Coated corn dogs.** Bold coatings like our Hot Cheetos Corn Dog, Potato Corn Dog or the fall Blue Takis Corn Dog already bring plenty of flavor, so go light and taste before you add more.

## Why the finish works better on a fresh corn dog

Toppings stick and stay crisp best on a shell that just came out of the fryer. That is why every Korean corn dog we serve is battered when you order and fried in 100 percent avocado oil. The shell is hot enough to hold the sugar and clean tasting enough that the sauce, not the oil, is what you notice.
